After Dawson City, we finally hit the Dempster Highway. Sure, we had to run a few last errands and fill up on gas first, but then we were finally on our way. 880 km toward the Arctic Ocean!

We knew this stretch would be exciting. No paved roads, no gas stations until Eagle Plains, and very little infrastructure otherwise. But we were looking forward to wonderful scenery and encounters along the way. Our first destination here was Tombstone Territorial Park.
It’s not quite the size of a national park, but it’s still one of the larger parks in the Yukon.
Since we no longer had any problems with any of our vehicles, we planned to reach the park within a day. Fortunately, that went off without a hitch.

We’d been warned in advance. The Dempster Highway isn’t a “highway” in the traditional sense. In Germany, we’d describe this road as a well-maintained dirt road.

<DirectusImage assetId="8fa44a9c-f8fb-4e60-a695-9869997f0b1e.jpeg" />

The Dempster turned out to be just as narrow and dusty as we’d expected. It’s also recommended to be cautious around oncoming trucks. Since they usually travel very fast (90–100 km/h), it’s not uncommon for passing vehicles to kick up rocks. To avoid this, it’s best to stop, pull over to the right, and wait until the truck has passed.

We were worried that, because it was the peak tourist season, we wouldn’t be able to get a spot at the park’s campground. However, those fears didn’t materialize, and we got great spots for our vehicles, so we were able to stay for two nights.

We took advantage of our early arrival at the campground to go on a short hike in the afternoon ([Tr’ondëk Dǫ’ Trail](https://yukon.ca/en/outdoor-recreation-and-wildlife/camping/hike-and-camp-tombstone-territorial-park#nav-road-accessible-hiking-trails)).
The real hikes were planned for the coming days. Since we stayed at Tombstone Park for two nights, we had set aside the middle day entirely for hiking and the day of departure for a hike along the rest of the trail.
Our hikes included the Goldenside, Grizzly Ridge, and Beaver Pond Trails.
The landscapes are truly indescribable, so we’ll just let the pictures speak for themselves here.

<DirectusGallery folderName="69 Tombstone" />

On August 13, we drove through Tombstone and continued along Engineer Creek toward Eagle Plains. We were slowly getting used to the road conditions and were no longer driving just 50–60 km/h, as we had at the beginning, but sometimes even 80 km/h.

When we arrived in Eagle Plains, we were very surprised at how sparse the “town” was. Perched on a mountaintop, it consists of a hotel/motel with a bar and a restaurant, a few RV campsites, and a gas station.
Although the gas station is basically just a shipping container filled with gas, with a payment terminal bolted onto it.
Still, we have to say, we were very happy to finally get burgers there again. We were also “lucky” that there was no working water on the premises, so we were able to spend the night at the RV parking lot for free.
It was basically one last chance to refuel (for us and for the car) before we cross the Arctic Circle and really head into the far north!
